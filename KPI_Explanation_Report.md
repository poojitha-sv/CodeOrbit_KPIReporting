# KPI Report - Explanation

## Business Scenario
I'm treating this as a small retail store that sells Electronics, Furniture, and Stationery, both in-store and online, and wants a quick way to check its overall health.

## The 5 KPIs I Chose

### 1. Total Revenue - $1,845.24
This is the most basic and most important number for any business - how much money came in. Without this, none of the other KPIs mean much on their own.

### 2. Average Order Value (AOV) - $76.89
This tells you how much a typical customer spends per order. It matters because it's often easier and cheaper to increase revenue by getting existing customers to spend a bit more per order (through bundles or upsells) than to constantly find new customers.

### 3. Repeat Customer Rate - 50.0%
Half of all orders in this sample came from returning customers. This matters because repeat customers are proof that people liked their first experience enough to come back - and they're usually cheaper to sell to than acquiring a brand new customer through ads or marketing.

### 4. Top Category Revenue Share - 47.6% (Furniture)
Almost half of all revenue comes from just one category, Furniture. This is useful to know because it shows the business is fairly dependent on that one category - if something disrupted Furniture sales (a supplier issue, a trend shift), it would hit the business hard. It also tells you where to focus marketing and stock investment.

### 5. Digital Payment Adoption Rate - 75.0%
Three out of every four orders were paid using Card or UPI rather than Cash. This matters for operational reasons - it tells the business where to invest in reliable payment processing, and it can also hint at the type of customer they're attracting (digital payments are often linked to younger or more convenience-focused shoppers).

## Why These 5 Specifically
I picked a mix on purpose - one overall health metric (Revenue), one spending-behavior metric (AOV), one loyalty metric (Repeat Rate), one product-strategy metric (Category Share), and one operations metric (Payment Method). Together they give a rounded view of the business rather than just looking at sales from one angle.
